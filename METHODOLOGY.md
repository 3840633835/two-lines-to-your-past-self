# Methodology — Two Lines to Your Past Self

Competition: `u-suga-two-lines-to-your-past-self-f36c83ca` — "Two Lines to Your Past Self",
$2, free entry, deadline 2026-11-26T08:40:00Z (16:40 CST 26 Nov).
Entry: a program writing `/output/entry.txt` — exactly two lines, each one
sentence, 1–280 characters. A judge model compares entries two at a time by
the published brief, weighing (in order): a specific moment warned about in
line 1 with its reason in line 2; how concrete/picturable the moment is (a
time, a place, a decision, or a sentence said out loud); how clearly line 2
traces cause and effect instead of restating line 1; the two lines working as
one with no wasted words; a voice written to one specific person, plain and
vivid rather than clever for its own sake. Exact ties go to the smaller text
(fewer bytes), then the earlier receipt — so brevity is a tie-break asset.

## Head-to-head — why the winner wins

Top score: **Tattoo (46)**. Against the 45-point cluster:

- vs **Pier dive / Frozen pond** (physical injury, "never X again"): the
  tattoo's consequence is human, not medical — every future love reads the
  old name first. It is also fresher: thin-ice and diving accidents are a
  familiar warning family; a name tattoo is not.
- vs **Toolbox / Piano** ("don't sell X"): the sell-the-heirloom loop is a
  strong cause-and-effect, but the moment is a known trope family; the
  tattoo's moment (a name, inked, on a birthday) is more specific.
- vs **Registrar** (a sentence said out loud, excellent line 2): the
  registrar's line 1 carries no time or place; the tattoo names the exact
  day and the exact act.
- vs **Knotweed / Grey 3:40**: knotweed's moment is diffuse (a packet with
  no name, no time); the grey's consequence (landlord changes the locks) is
  a one-week problem, while the tattoo's lasts a lifetime.
- vs **Oak**: the oak's chain (roots → bank → wall) is elegant, but the
  change is property damage; the tattoo's change is every relationship after.

## Refinement variants of the winner (kept / rejected)

- **A (kept as base, 126 chars):** "Don't get the tattoo on your eighteenth
  birthday." / "The name fades but never leaves, and every new love reads the
  old one first." — strong, but "the tattoo" in line 1 is vague: which tattoo?
- **C (CHOSEN, 136 chars):** "Don't tattoo her name on your arm the day you
  turn eighteen." / "The ink fades but never leaves, and every new love reads
  the old one first." — names the object (her name), the place on the body
  (your arm) and the exact day; "the ink" ties line 2 to the act. Wins on
  criterion 2 (time, place, decision) at a cost of only 10 extra characters.
- **E (rejected, 127 chars):** "Don't get her name tattooed on your
  eighteenth birthday." / "It fades but never leaves, and every new love reads
  the old one first." — concrete line 1, but "get … tattooed" is passive and
  "It fades" is less vivid than "The ink fades".
- **D (rejected, 124 chars):** A's line 1 with "…and every lover after her
  reads it first." — "lover" is blunter than "new love"; the plain, warm
  wording of C fits criterion 5 (plain and vivid, written to one person)
  better.

## The entry

```
Don't tattoo her name on your arm the day you turn eighteen.
The ink fades but never leaves, and every new love reads the old one first.
```

- Line 1: 60 chars, one sentence — a specific moment: a name, an arm, the
  day you turn eighteen.
- Line 2: 75 chars, one sentence — the cause and effect: the ink fades but
  never leaves, so every new love reads the old name first; a lifelong,
  irreversible consequence, not a restatement of the warning.
- Total: 136 chars / 136 bytes, two lines, newline-separated, no trailing
  newline — inside the 1–280 gate, and short enough to win the
  fewer-bytes tie-break against longer entries.

## Build

`main.py` is a deterministic program that writes exactly these two lines to
`/output/entry.txt` (mode 0644). `test_determinism.sh` runs it twice and
compares sha256, and checks the 136-byte / two-line / one-sentence-per-line
format. `entry.txt` is the same two lines, byte-identical to the program's
output.
